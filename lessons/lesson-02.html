Warm-up

Your German eyes

Read these words aloud. Don’t listen first!

but · my · sit · cat · stop · not · pen · time

Now listen. Did you read them the English way?

Why does this happen?

You learned German first, so your eyes read English words with German rules. The letters are the same, but many sounds are different.

WordGerman eyes say…English says…Why?
buːt “boot”bʌtEnglish u in short words is not German u
miː “me”maɪEnglish y at the end often says ai
zɪtsɪtEnglish s at the start is never z
ʃtɔp “shtop”stɑpno sh in st / sp
tiːmə “tee-meh”taɪmthe e at the end is silent
The good news: English spelling has rules too. In this lesson you learn the first nine sounds: i t s a c n o p e. Later lessons fix u (but) and y (my).
2
Your German helps

Short vowels

In a short word like c-a-t or p-e-n, the vowel is short.

German has short vowels too — in bitte, Bett, Mann. Three English short vowels are almost the same as German ones. Two are new.

LetterSoundEnglishLike German…
i/ɪ/bitte, ist ✅
e/e/Bett, essen ✅
o/ɑ/Mann, hat ✅ — yes, an a!
a/æ/— new! between a and ä
u/ʌ/— new! (a later lesson)
Letter name ≠ letter sound. The name of A is “ay”, but in cat it says /æ/. To read a word, you need the sounds.

short i — as in it

How to say it

Short and relaxed. Don’t smile, don’t stretch the lips. It is the i in German bitte, Mitte, ist.

Listen and repeat

German trap: “ie”Your German eyes want a long “ie” /iː/. In a short English word, i is always short: is not , is not . The long sound is usually spelled ee or ea.
Tt
/t/
t — as in top

How to say it

The tip of your tongue is behind your top teeth. Then a short puff of air: t! The same as German. Easy!

Listen and repeat

Say the t at the end clearly: it, sit, cat. th (this, think) is a different sound — a later lesson.

Meet the sounds

s and a

Ss
/s/
the snake sound — as in sun

How to say it

Teeth close together, air hisses out: sssss. Touch your throat: no buzz! (The buzzing sound is /z/.)

Listen and repeat

German trap 1: s + vowelIn German, s before a vowel buzzes: Sonne, sehen. In English, s at the start of a word never buzzes: is not , is not .
German trap 2: st and spIn German, Stein, Sport start with “sht”, “shp”. In English, st and sp are a clean s + t, s + p: .
Small exception: in is, as, has, his the s buzzes like /z/ . Don’t say “iss”!
Aa
/æ/
short a — as in cat

How to say it

This sound is not in German. Open your mouth wide (two fingers fit between your teeth), keep your tongue low and forward. It’s between German a and ä. Short!

Listen and repeat

German trap: a / eDon’t say a German “a” or an “e”. Listen: or , or , or .
The name of the letter A is “ay”, but in short words it says /æ/.

How to say it

The back of your tongue goes up, then a puff of air: k! Usually the letter c sounds just like k.

Rule: c + a, o, u → /k/ · c + e, i, y → /s/ · never “ts”!

o and p

Oo
/ɑ/
short o — as in hot

How to say it

Open your mouth wide and relax, like at the doctor’s: “Say ah!” It is close to the a in German Mann, hat. Yes: English hot sounds almost like German hat!

Listen and repeat

German trap: long “oh”German eyes read o as “oh”, like in Boot, Ofen. In a short English word, o is an open “ah”: is not , is not .
At the end of short words, o says its name /oʊ/: — not a German “oh”: the sound glides to “u”.
Pp
/p/
p — as in pen

How to say it

Lips together, then a strong puff of air. Hold a sheet of paper in front of your mouth: it should move!

Listen and repeat

German trap again: spClean s + p, no “shp”: .

Boss level

The magic silent e

In German you say every e: Name, Hose, Katze. In English, an e at the end is usually silent…

…but it has a job: it makes the vowel before it say its name. a → “ay”, i → “eye”, o → “oh”, e → “ee”.

Final reading

Stan and the tent

Read aloud

Read the story aloud. Then listen to each part and compare. Then record the whole story.

Stan is ten. He has a tent. It is a big black tent.

Stan pins his tent in a spot in the pines. In the tent, he has a map, a pen, a lamp and a snack.

It is hot. Stan naps. Then – tap, tap, tap! Is it a cat? Is it a dog? It is not a pet. It is an ant. No, it is ten ants!

The ants grab the snack and trot off. Stan is sad. Then he spots a red apple in his bag. Stan is glad. The end.

Check yourself: short vowels in Stan, ten, big, not, spot? Clean st, sp? Silent e in pines? A snake s in sad, snack?
