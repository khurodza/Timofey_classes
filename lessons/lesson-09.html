Pronunciation · Student’s Book p. 120 · 1.03–1.04

/h/ or /w/ in question words

Exercise 1 · 1.03

Read and listen to the questions.

How old are you?

Where are you from?

What’s your favourite food?

Who’s your favourite football player?

Why do you like him?

Two sounds, not three!Who and How start with /h/ — just a soft breath, like warming your hands. Not a hard Russian «х»!
What, Where, Why start with /w/ — round your lips like for «у». Not «в»: your teeth don’t touch your lip.
Careful: Who is /huː/ — the w is silent.

RULE: The verb to be changes for different subject pronouns.

I am American.

You / We / They 4 American.

He / She / It 5 American.

We often use contracted forms after pronouns:
I am = I’m · You / We / They are = You’re / We’re / They’re · He / She / It is = He’s / She’s / It’s

Look!SingularPlural
1stI am → I’mwe are → we’re
2ndyou are → you’reyou are → you’re
3rdhe / she / it is → he’s / she’s / it’sthey are → they’re
Say it short — and softIn speaking we nearly always say the short form: . Don’t make am / is / are strong.
But with names, no short are: Liam and Ben are friends, not Liam and Ben’re.

Meet the sounds

o and p

Oo
/ɑ/
short o — as in hot

How to say it

Open your mouth wide and relax, like at the doctor’s: “Say ah!” It is close to the a in German Mann, hat. Yes: English hot sounds almost like German hat!

Listen and repeat

German trap: long “oh”German eyes read o as “oh”, like in Boot, Ofen. In a short English word, o is an open “ah”: is not , is not .
At the end of short words, o says its name /oʊ/: — not a German “oh”: the sound glides to “u”.
Pp
/p/
p — as in pen

How to say it

Lips together, then a strong puff of air. Hold a sheet of paper in front of your mouth: it should move!

Listen and repeat

German trap again: spClean s + p, no “shp”: .
