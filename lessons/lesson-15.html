A digraph is a team of two letters that make one sound.

Don’t read the letters one by one: s + h is not “s-h”. Together they are one new sound. Today: sh → ch → ph → ck → th.

ph
/f/
as in photo
p+h→ph=/f/

How to say it

Easy: ph is just f. Top teeth on your bottom lip and blow. There is no “p” in it at all!

Key words

More words: listen and repeat

German trap: Foto ≠ photoThese words come from Greek. Modern German often writes f (Foto, Telefon, Elefant, Delfin), but English keeps ph: , , , .
Never say “p-hoto” or “p-hone”. One sound: /f/.

ck
/k/
as in duck
c+k→ck=/k/

How to say it

Two letters, but only one short k. Just like German Rock, dick, Jacke. The vowel before ck is always short.

Key words

More words: listen and repeat

Where does ck live?ck comes right after one short vowel, in the middle or at the end of a word: back, rocket. It is never at the start of a word.
k or ck?After a long vowel, two vowel letters or a consonant, write only k: .

th
/ð/
buzzing th, as in father
t+h→th=/ð/

How to say it

The tongue is in the same place: between your teeth. Now switch your voice on. Put a hand on your throat: it buzzes, like a bee behind your teeth.

Key words

More words: listen and repeat

German trap: not z, not dKeep the tongue between your teeth:
or
or
or
How do I know which th?Buzzing /ð/: small grammar words (the, this, that, these, those, they, there, then) and family words with -ther (mother, father, brother). Quiet /θ/: most other words (think, three, month, maths).
